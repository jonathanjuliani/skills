## Taken

- Marketing copy is type-led. The headline and a short proof ("AI where your team works", answers with citations) carry the page; illustration is not required for the claim.
- The workspace is framed as a system of record, not as a feature grid. One place for context is the product idea, and the layout should say that.
- The live surface in this read was a focused login: one labelled field, one primary Continue, SSO options as a clearly secondary row. Labels persist. Placeholders do not replace them.
- New user is a text link, not a second button of equal weight.
- One family. Spacing is a 4/8 base with a tight hairline that shows up constantly on chrome. Modest radius: smaller on buttons, slightly larger on cards.

## Rejected

- The wordmark, the illustration style, the named family, and the primary-button hue. Those are identity.
- Treating a login-wall scrape as the workspace system. The extractor saw sign-in, not the product.
- Redirecting a marketing URL to login without a public read. A first-time visitor cannot judge the product from a sign-in form.
- A wall of SSO providers given equal visual weight. Rank the one most of this audience will use, and keep the rest quieter.

## Not applicable here

- Store-only pass. Apply when a product is named. The login pattern transfers to any focused sign-in. The marketing read transfers to editorial product pages, not to dense operator tools.
