## Taken

- The work is the argument. A reel of actual pieces sits under a short claim, which is the honest version of "show, don't tell" for a studio.
- One primary action (Book a Call). The rest of the page is proof and FAQ.
- Audience is named by job (creative directors, marketing leaders, founders), each with a different reason. That is ranking by reader, not a wall of equal personas.
- Copy is specific about the operating model (a dedicated team, a calendar, a review loop), which is what this buyer is comparing.
- A sans for the claim, plus a novelty display used as decoration. Many weights and sizes in the CSS; the page still reads as a few. Spacing is noisy. Small-to-medium radius; shadows are plentiful because the reel needs depth.

## Rejected

- The filmstrip frame. Signature layout. Leave it.
- The wordmark, the accent hue in the reel titles, and the novelty display face. Identity.
- The type-size dump from the scrape. Keep the few sizes you can see.
- Logo walls as "trusted by" without a named outcome. The reel already did the proof.

## Not applicable here

- Store-only pass. Apply when a product is named. A studio marketing page is not a product application, and a filmstrip is not a video player pattern to reuse.
