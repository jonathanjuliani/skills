## Taken

- Scroll is the product. Type morphs and sections ease as the user moves, so smoothing is evidence rather than a claim in a paragraph.
- The page argues for one feel across wheel, trackpad, and touch, and for keeping scroll-linked layers in sync. Those are the transferable reasons to smooth, not a taste for inertia.
- Accessibility is part of the claim: no hijacked scrollbar, first paint still a document. Smooth scroll that cannot be skipped is the failure this product is positioning against.
- Copy is specific about the job ("keep WebGL and the DOM perfectly in sync") rather than "buttery".

## Rejected

- The pink full-bleed type morph and the wordmark. Signature choreography, not a default for other products.
- Smooth scroll as atmosphere on a dense tool or a checkout. This audience is choosing a library; operators do not need inertia on a table.
- Implementation recipes (one-liner setup, plugin names). Record "scroll position equals feel", not the call that produced it.

## Not applicable here

- Store when a marketing or editorial page wants scroll as structure without a WebGL scene. Wrong for data tables, forms, and any surface where native scroll is the accessibility requirement.
