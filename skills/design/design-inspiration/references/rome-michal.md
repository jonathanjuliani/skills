## Taken

- The headline is interrupted by a real product screenshot, then finished. Proof sits inside the claim, which is how a product designer shows the work instead of describing it.
- Copy is a point of view ("Pretty is the easy part") plus facts (years, location, discipline). Recruiter scanning needs both in one screen.
- Work entries are named by the system, not by a metaphor, and each carries a one-line outcome.
- Chrome is name, menu, and the work. Nothing else on first paint.
- One family, two weights. Spacing in the scrape is full of fractional computed values; the page still reads as a few large steps. Radius is almost only pills.

## Rejected

- Scroll-entrance on the headline. A hiring manager who arrives mid-animation gets an unfinished sentence.
- The portrait in the header as a layout to copy. A face is identity.
- Motion that splits a sentence across a delayed image. If the image is the proof, it should be there when the words are.
- Fractional spacing from the scrape. Pill-only radius is a portfolio motif, not a form pattern.

## Not applicable here

- Store-only pass. Apply when a product is named. A portfolio split-headline is not a pattern for a product application.
