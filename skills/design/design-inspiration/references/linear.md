## Taken

- The product is the argument. A plausible issue, with real activity and labels, sits under a short headline so a buyer can judge density before they sign up.
- List and detail on the same view. Navigation names places (Inbox, Issues, Reviews), the selected row is obvious, and the detail pane carries the work.
- Type is few sizes and two weights. Hierarchy comes from space and position, not from extra weights. One sans for UI, a mono only for code and timestamps.
- Colour is almost all neutral. Hue is reserved for status and selection, which is how a dense tool stays scannable for hours.
- Spacing is a 4/8 base with hairline gaps on chrome, not a unique gap per component. Corners stay small on chrome. Shadows lift panels; they are not decoration.
- Skip to content is present. Keyboard-first products advertise that they take focus seriously.
- One primary action in the chrome (Sign up), with Log in visibly secondary.

## Rejected

- The wordmark, the brand hue, and the named families. Those are identity.
- Extracted spacing leftovers (odd steps that never appear as a rhythm on the page). The scrape is evidence of shipped CSS, not a scale to import.
- Treating the dark canvas as a theme to copy. Dark here is their product language, not a requirement of density.
- Ambient motion on the marketing canvas. The issue view is evidence; looping it as theatre is not.

## Not applicable here

- Store-only pass. Apply when a product is named. This read is for teams who live in a tracker, not for a marketing page that needs to persuade a stranger in one scroll.
