## Taken

- Price and scope sit in the first claim. "150+ lab tests", "$349 per year" is the argument, not a vague promise of better health.
- One primary membership action repeats ("Become a member" / "Get started"). Secondary is informational ("See what we test"), not a competing conversion.
- The membership block restates the same numbers as a checklist (biomarkers, scores, plan, AI + care team, turnaround, lab access). Pricing is a section, not a buried link.
- Social proof is specific: named member stories with ages, outcome sentences, and a Trustpilot score with review count.
- FAQ answers objections that would otherwise kill the purchase (cancel anytime, how often to test, when results arrive) instead of repeating the hero.

## Rejected

- The wordmark and the vermillion accent as a brand to copy. Identity.
- Influencer follower counts as the main trust signal. Useful for their audience; weak for a procurement or ops tool.
- Treating the dark product photography and body imagery as a theme to import. That is their health-brand language.

## Not applicable here

- Store when the brief needs pricing-forward consumer membership marketing. Wrong reference for dense admin tools or text-only B2B trust pages that should not lead with a yearly price.
