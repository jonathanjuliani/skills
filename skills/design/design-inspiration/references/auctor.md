## Taken

- Headline does one job ("One System, Every Implementation"). Subhead explains the job-to-be-done; the product canvas underneath is the evidence, not a second headline.
- One primary action (Book a Demo). Login stays secondary in the chrome.
- The canvas shows a plausible work state (greeting, composer with @ and /, saved skills as named jobs). Buyers can judge density before a call.
- Problem framing is concrete: siloed docs, memory, disagreement six months later. The product is positioned as the fix for that failure, not as a feature list first.
- Lifecycle tabs (Pre-Sales / Post-Sales) segment one product story without splitting into competing CTAs.

## Rejected

- The wordmark and the top event banner colour as brand to copy.
- Serif display as a required "enterprise" look. It works for their voice; the transferable choice is a short claim plus product proof.
- Logo walls of customers as a substitute for a named outcome. Their quotes carry more than the mark row.

## Not applicable here

- Store when the surface is B2B marketing for implementation or delivery software and a live canvas helps. Wrong for consumer membership pricing pages or single-task flows that should remove everything but the form.
