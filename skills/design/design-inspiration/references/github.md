## Taken

- The product demo is real code, not an illustration of coding. Plausible diffs and chat about a refactor prove the tool exists.
- Sign-up is the primary conversion and it is inline: email field plus one action, with a secondary download. The field is labelled by its placeholder role and sits next to the action, not in a later section.
- Skip to content, pause on animation, and pause on video. A motion-heavy developer page still lets you stop it.
- Claims carry numbers (time to restore, secrets stopped). Adjectives about speed are backed or they are absent.
- Sections are named by job (code, plan, collaborate, automate, secure), which is how a large platform stays a map instead of a pile.
- One family with a mono cut. The CSS carries many variable weights; the page still reads as a few sizes. Spacing is 4/8 with hairlines. Modest radius on controls.

## Rejected

- The inverted-cat mark, the green accent, and the named families. Those are identity.
- Hundreds of CSS colour variables in the scrape. That dump is not a palette to import. Fractional spacing leftovers are computed CSS, not tokens.
- The purple glow around the product frame. A gradient standing in for a decision, and it costs contrast on the demo.
- Two competing actions of similar weight in the hero. Download is useful; it should not match Sign up.

## Not applicable here

- Store-only pass. Apply when a product is named. This is a developer-platform marketing page. The dense application GitHub is famous for is behind sign-in and was not this read.
