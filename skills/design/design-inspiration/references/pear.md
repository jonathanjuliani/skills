## Taken

- The business model is the hero. "No fees. A share of the upside." lands before case studies, because the terms decide the deal.
- One persistent action (Apply / Request Partnership). The page never offers a competing secondary conversion.
- FAQ answers the objections that kill revenue-share deals (what it costs, what share, how revenue is measured, how long) in plain sentences, not marketing adjectives.
- Explicit "we say no" criteria (pre-revenue, hourly rentals, Friday deadlines) builds trust by narrowing fit instead of claiming everyone.
- The apply block is a focused single task: name, work email, what you want to grow, one send button. Everything else supports that decision.

## Rejected

- Classical pear-tree imagery as a signature to copy. The transferable idea is lead with terms, not the illustration style.
- Spaced letter-display as a required editorial look. It is their voice; the structure (claim → terms → who fits → apply) is what transfers.
- Multiple CTAs ("Book a call", "See work", "Apply") that would dilute the partnership funnel.

## Not applicable here

- Store when the brief needs text-led marketing or a single apply/request task. Wrong for product-led SaaS that should show the UI first, or for archives that should not funnel into a form.
