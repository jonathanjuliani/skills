## Taken

- Search is the primary action, placed on the task, not delayed until after a brand story. People arrive with a job to do.
- Popular tasks are named as the thing you came to do (sign in to tax, check a pension), never as a campaign headline.
- Topics are a labelled list with a one-line scope under each. Equal cards would hide that Benefits is not the same kind of need as News.
- Skip to main content, a persistent search field, and cookie Accept/Reject as two clear choices. Accessibility-critical surfaces put those in the chrome, not in a modal over the work.
- Copy is specific and plain. The page tells you what you can do, not how you should feel.
- Motion is absent. For this audience, movement would cost trust and time.
- One family, two weights only. Spacing follows a 5-based public-service rhythm, not a 4/8 SaaS scale. Corners are sharp. Shadows are almost unused.

## Rejected

- The crown, the wordmark, and the government typeface. Those are identity, and they are protected marks besides.
- Flattening this to an 8px scale because an extractor labelled it that way. The live rhythm is 5-based. Fallback sans in the scrape is not a second family.
- The header colour as a brand to reuse. The structure (search, popular, topics) transfers; the colour does not.
- Treating equal visual weight of cookie Accept and Reject as a marketing-CTA pattern. Consent is not conversion. Rank marketing actions; do not rank legal choices the same way.

## Not applicable here

- Store-only pass. Apply when a product is named. This is the reference for accessibility-critical and trust-first work, not for a portfolio or a cinematic brand.
