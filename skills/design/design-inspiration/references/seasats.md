## Taken

- Specs are the argument. Persistence, speed, payload, and sea state appear as concrete numbers next to each vessel, not as adjectives.
- The vessel is a photograph you can stay with. Hero image of the hardware, then click-and-drag missions, so image motion is inspection rather than a wallpaper loop.
- A side index of named sections is the scroll map. Scroll is wayfinding through capabilities and SKUs, not a new trick per block.
- Product family is filterable (Commercial / Defense / Science; Lightfish / Quickfish / Heavyfish) so a buyer can narrow without leaving the page.
- Mission outcomes are stated as detectable unknowns (ghost fleet, hurricanes, illegal fishing) before features, which frames why autonomy matters.
- Capability stacks repeat a three-beat structure (Deploy / Detect / React) so a long page stays scannable.
- Press links carry specific outcomes (Taiwan Strait transit, eight-day trial, Series A) instead of a logo wall alone.

## Rejected

- Spaced-letter UI chrome and theatrical scroll metrics as a style to import. Award theatre; the transferable craft is ranked specs and mission framing.
- Copying the vertical retractable menu as a signature navigation pattern. Unusual nav is their experiment, not a convention.
- Brand hues from the Awwwards palette scrape. Structure of the vessel brief transfers; the colours do not.

## Not applicable here

- Store when marketing complex hardware or multi-SKU products to expert buyers. Wrong for consumer membership pricing or text-only trust pages without a physical product to show.
