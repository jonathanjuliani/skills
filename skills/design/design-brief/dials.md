# Dials

Three dials turn a design read into decisions. They are set once, out loud, as part of the read, and they gate every layout, movement and spacing choice that follows. The project outranks this file everywhere: where an existing screen already answers a dial, that answer wins.

Snapshot date: 2026-09. This is convention, not standard, unlike the accessibility numbers in `frontend-craft`'s design defaults, which do not drift.

## Read to dials

| Read | Variance | Motion | Density |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Internal tool, dashboard, admin, data table | low | low | high |
| Product UI, app screens, settings | low to medium | low to medium | medium |
| Marketing page, landing, pricing | medium to high | medium | low |
| Portfolio, editorial, brand-led | high | medium to high | low |
| Regulated, public sector, accessibility-critical | low | minimal | medium |

Higher variance means asymmetry, overlap, and a looser grid. Higher motion means more transition and entrance work. Higher density means tighter spacing and more information per screen. When the read is ambiguous between two rows, take the lower-variance one: a restrained interface that is wrong reads as plain, an exuberant one that is wrong reads as broken.

## Named motion levels

When stating the brief or tagging a reference, prefer these labels over only "low/medium/high". Call the Skill tool with "design-inspiration" for the full read of motion craft; the seed indexes captures by these levels.

| Dial (table above) | Named level | Short test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| minimal / low | **minimal** | Almost nothing moves; scroll does not choreograph |
| low to medium / medium | **purposeful** | Motion explains a state change; nothing ambient loops |
| medium to high | **cinematic** | Scroll, timeline, or ambient motion is part of first impression |

Wrong level at the wrong density is worse than a plain interface. When unsure, take the lower named level.
